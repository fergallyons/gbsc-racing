Helm Steers to the target angle and boat speed, not to a number on the compass.

StartHold the line, judge time/distance, call the manoeuvre early.
UpwindSteer the AWA band, not height — 25–26° in 4–6kt tightening to 18–21° as it builds. Speed first, then point.
RoundingsSmooth entry and exit — don't sacrifice speed for a tight line.
DownwindSteer pressure and angle for VMG, working with the trimmer on kite load.
FinishLine judgment; cover or extend exactly as tactician calls it.

Main Trimmer Runs the German mainsheet, traveller and backstay — the boat's throttle.
TravellerBackstayOuthaul / Cunningham / Vang
Pre-StartTraveller to start position, backstay/outhaul/cunningham set per the trim card for the band.
StartTraveller and sheet for acceleration off the line — ease, build, then trim on.
UpwindGust: traveller down → backstay on → ease sheet. Lull: reverse the order. Traveller leads, not the sheet.
RoundingsEase/dump for the bear-away, trim back on for the next beat.
DownwindVang and leech control; stay alert for the mainsheet fouling the jib sheet or car near the mast.
FinishFinal trim call into the line.
Watch forBackstay on before 8kt and mainsheet-before-traveller in a gust are this season's two recurring mistakes. The German mainsheet gear is also suspected to be limiting how far forward the jib car can go — see the Jib Trimmer card.
Calls you make: "Traveller down." "Backstay 20%." "Good speed." "Depowered."
Jib Trimmer Runs the genoa sheet, car and inhauler — the slot and the pointing.
Jib carInhaulerTelltales
Pre-StartCar for the band — FWD 1 in 4–8kt, Std 3 mid-range, AFT 1/2 in breeze. Inhauler set, not off above 8kt.
StartTrim for acceleration — ease, build speed, then trim on.
UpwindWatch all three telltales top to bottom; call out which one breaks first.
RoundingsEase for the bear-away, grind on hard for the next beat.
DownwindStaysail sheet if it's set — dedicated focus through every gybe.
FinishHold the groove into the line.
Watch forInhauler has been under-used in 8–16kt all season — use it. Separately, there's an open theory that the German mainsheet gear limits how far forward the car can go; don't chase that fix until it's been tested cleanly (KB §23).
Calls you make: "Tell-tales good." "More inhauler." "Car aft one." "Slot open."
Pit Runs the halyards — every hoist and drop passes through here.
HalyardsReef linesHoist / Drop
Pre-StartKite halyard attached and led correctly. If the staysail's planned, confirm the inner forestay is ready.
StartHalyards ready for the first hoist of the race.
UpwindStand by and prep the kite hoist approaching the top mark.
RoundingsCall hoist/drop timing, manage halyard tension through the rounding.
DownwindManage kite halyard and staysail halyard/reef if it's flying.

Watch forThe staysail is hanked on, not furling — striking it is a manual bag-and-hank job. Build in the extra time rather than assuming it can be rolled away fast if the breeze comes up.

This season's recurring mistakes

  • Sailing to height, not speed
  • Traveller too low below 10kt
  • Mainsheet before traveller in a gust
  • Backstay on before 8kt
  • AWA too wide in 12–20kt — it should tighten as wind builds, not ease
  • Chasing every puff on the AWA number instead of holding a groove
  • Jib car left aft in light air
  • Inhauler not used in 8–16kt
  • Ignoring tell-tales
  • Poor communication through tacks
  • AWA sensor trusted without checking it first
  • Mux TWA Conversions tab skipped before racing
