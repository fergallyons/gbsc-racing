What SailPro builds that Race Ops doesn't

A deep dive on sailpro.app — a small (~5K installs) mobile-first sailing platform spanning personal race instruments, a club race-control product, and a gamified activity layer. This note separates what's actually built for club race-day ops (SailPro's closest overlap with Race Ops) from what's aimed at individual racers, and ends with concrete, gbsc-racing-specific recommendations.

Lay of the land

Three products, one brand

SailPro's marketing site blurs this together, but the actual product splits cleanly into three tiers of relevance to a club-ops platform like Race Ops:

Club-facing
RaceControlRaceHubFleetStart
A browser-based dashboard for the race officer: create races, build courses on a map, run a synchronised start, track the fleet live by phone GPS, replay after racing, and share a public viewer link. This is the direct competitor to Race Ops — see the recommendations below.
Personal tools
TacticianAnchorMeBoatHubCrewTalkRacing Simulator
In-race instruments (compass, laylines, start-line timing), an anchor watch alarm, a per-boat tuning/setup logbook, in-app crew voice rooms, and a standalone training simulator. Built for an individual racer's own phone, not for a club running race night — off-mission for Race Ops.
Engagement
SailPro XPBerthABoat
A cross-club points/leaderboard/challenge layer for "sailing activity" beyond race day, plus an unrelated marina-berth booking marketplace. XP's underlying idea (reward turning up, not just finishing well) is the one piece worth borrowing — see Recommendation 4.

Recommendations

Worth building

Ranked by how concrete the gap is against our own code, not by how impressive the feature sounds on SailPro's landing page.

1 · Push race-status changes instead of polling
High

SailPro's FleetStart pitch is exactly this: "changes made by the Race Officer are reflected across every competitor's device" the moment they happen. Our start sequence polls for postpone/recall/abandon on a timer rather than pushing them — a boat could be up to 15 seconds behind an RO's decision to postpone or abandon, at exactly the moment that matters most.

Where: refreshStartSeqData() is driven by setInterval(refreshStartSeqData, 15000) at app.js:13226 — confirmed no Supabase Realtime channel or push is wired to start-sequence state today. The countdown display itself is fine (it ticks locally off a shared timestamp) — it's specifically RO actions mid-sequence that lag.
2 · Attach a track replay clip to a filed protest
High

SailPro doesn't have protest filing at all — but it explicitly sells its race replay for "protest discussions, safety review." We have the opposite gap: a real protest-filing workflow (RRS 61.2, redress, scoring enquiry) and a real GPS track replay with a scrubber, and the two don't know about each other. Letting a skipper or RO jump from a filed protest straight to the relevant few minutes of track replay would beat what either product does alone.

Where: submitProtest() (app.js:14001) and the race tracker's replay/scrubber (raceTrackerPanel, trackerScrubber) are entirely separate code paths today — no shared timestamp or boat reference between them.
3 · Save a built course as a reusable template
Medium

SailPro clubs can "create marks, save regular club marks, build race courses and save those courses as templates" for faster weekly reuse. Our course-card mode already ships a pre-loaded library, but that's developer-curated per club, not something an RO builds once (in mark-builder or laid-course mode) and reuses next Wednesday without rebuilding it.

Where: sbSaveCourse() (app.js:473) persists a course per-race; there's no separate "save as template" / "load from template" step in that flow today.
4 · Turnout-based recognition, not just results
Medium

SailPro XP's actual mechanism (points/leaderboard/challenges for showing up, not for winning) is a genuinely different lever than results-based standings — aimed at midweek turnout, not competitive standing. For a club already running "Crew Wanted / Available Crew" as a community feature, a light season-turnout badge or streak (races started, not races won) fits the same spirit without needing SailPro's full gamification machinery.

Why medium, not high: speculative — worth a conversation about whether GBSC-style clubs want this at all before designing it. Flagging the idea, not the implementation.
5 · Show the tracking sailor their own GPS signal quality
Medium

SailPro's competitor screen shows a plain reassurance while tracking: "GPS Excellent · accuracy 3.5m · updated 2 seconds ago." Small, but it directly answers the one question a sailor has after tapping "share my position" — is this actually working? Today our GPS agent gives no such feedback once tracking starts.

Where: confirmed no accuracy readout anywhere in the tracking UI (app.js — only internal use at line 1522 for the geolocation call options, never surfaced to the user).
Not worth chasing

Deliberately skipping

Named explicitly so a future pass doesn't re-litigate these — each is either off-mission for a club-ops platform or solves a problem this app's clubs don't have.

Racing Simulator, Tactician
Personal skill-building and in-race tactical instruments for an individual sailor's own phone — a different product category from running a club's race night.
BoatHub tuning logbook
Rig-tuning notes and performance history matter most to one-design fleets chasing tenths of a knot; GBSC-style clubs run mixed-handicap fleets where this has little payoff.
CrewTalk voice rooms
In-app walkie-talkie for crew. VHF is the actual norm on the water in Irish sailing clubs — an in-app substitute solves a problem nobody has.
Corporate banner-ad inventory
Part of SailPro's own SaaS revenue model (paid ad slots by tier). Race Ops already has a single-sponsor widget per club; building an ad marketplace serves a business model we don't have.
Proprietary tracking hardware resale
SailPro sells optional dedicated trackers alongside the phone-based option. Our GPS-agent-on-a-phone approach is the same low-cost model they lead with — no reason to add hardware.
